# Source register

Evidence checked November 1, 2026. These are dated observations, not a claim to have extracted the latest available survey wave.

## S1 What Work Does Generative AI Do

**author:** Bick, Blandin, Deming and Schumacher

**published:** 2026-09-01

**observation:** August 2024 to May 2026; task indexes August 2025 to May 2026

**url:** https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2026/sep/what-work-does-generative-ai-do

**finding:** The authors report adult generative-AI use rising from 45% to 62%, and worker use for jobs from 33% to 45%. Their task analysis finds broad reach with much shallower use of individual tasks.

**unit:** US adults ages 18-64 and workers; not firms or support departments

**limit:** Self-reported use is not sustained enterprise deployment, authorized use, or support-peer penetration. May observations are not November rates.

**locator:** Opening paragraphs and How Do We Measure AI Use at the Task Level

**accessed:** 2026-11-01

## S2 Large Firms With at Least 20 Employees Biggest AI Users

**author:** Grundy, Breaux and Khatiwoda, US Census Bureau

**published:** 2026-05-26

**observation:** 2025-12-14 to 2026-05-03

**url:** https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html

**finding:** BTOS reports AI use around 17-20% of US businesses during this period and 39.7% in Information on May 3. The question broadened in November 2025 to any business function.

**unit:** US businesses; all AI, past two weeks

**limit:** Not generative AI specifically; Information is broader than B2B software support. Do not combine with worker surveys or compare across the wording break as an unchanged series.

**locator:** US Business AI Use and AI Trends Across Sectors

**accessed:** 2026-11-01

## S3 Generative AI at Work

**author:** Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond

**published:** 2025-02-04; QJE May 2025, 140(2), 889-942

**observation:** Historical staggered deployment studied in the published paper

**url:** https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044

**finding:** A study of 5,172 support agents at one business-process software firm finds 15% more issues resolved per hour on average, with heterogeneous effects. Human agents could edit or ignore suggestions.

**unit:** Agents at one firm; productivity outcome, not a market diffusion rate

**limit:** Supports an application-level relative-advantage mechanism and an actual deployment. Does not estimate the percentage of peer firms adopting, local ROI, or autonomous-system performance.

**locator:** Abstract and Introduction; original full article checked at https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658

**accessed:** 2026-11-01

## S4 Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile

**author:** Autio et al., NIST

**published:** 2024-07-26

**observation:** July 2024 guidance; reference page checked 2026-11-01

**url:** https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1

**finding:** Voluntary cross-sector guidance identifies confabulation, privacy, human-AI configuration and component-integration risks, with suggested governance and evaluation actions.

**unit:** Risk guidance; not adoption statistics or law

**limit:** Using the profile is a proposed governance aid, not evidence of compliance, certification, current market diffusion or local control effectiveness.

**locator:** Sections 1, 2.2, 2.4, 2.7 and 2.12; original PDF checked at https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=958388

**accessed:** 2026-11-01

## F1 Diffusion of Innovations fifth edition

**author:** Everett M. Rogers

**published:** 2003

**observation:** Theory reference, not a current adoption observation

**url:** ISBN 9780743222099

**finding:** Use innovator, early adopter, early majority, late majority and laggard as relative adoption-timing categories; assess relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability and observability.

**unit:** Adoption units in a defined social system over time

**limit:** This package does not claim fresh access to the full book. Five-factor terminology was cross-checked against University of Florida IFAS WC089 search text; its full page had a redirect-loop error. No curve is fitted and no theoretical category percentage is treated as a measured market share.

**locator:** Rogers framework; instructional cross-check https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/wc089

**accessed:** 2026-11-01
